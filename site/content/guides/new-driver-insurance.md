**Quick answer:** young drivers pay the most for car insurance. A 16-year-old averages about **$10,387 a year** for full coverage, falling to about **$5,173 at 20** and **$3,044 at 25** ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/average-rates-by-age.aspx)). Adding a teen to a parent's policy is usually cheaper than a separate policy, and discounts for good grades, driver's ed and safe driving can cut the price further.

[[genie]]

## What young drivers pay, by age

Average yearly rates for a sample driver with a clean record ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/average-rates-by-age.aspx), 2026 data from Quadrant Information Services):

| Age | Full coverage | State minimum |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | $10,387 | $3,302 |
| 17 | $8,614 | $2,726 |
| 18 | $7,498 | $2,300 |
| 19 | $5,718 | $1,723 |
| 20 | $5,173 | $1,521 |
| 21 | $4,215 | $1,220 |
| 25 | $3,044 | $860 |

Full coverage here means 100/300/100 liability plus collision and comprehensive with $500 deductibles. Your price will differ by state, car, insurer and driving record. Teen boys usually pay more than teen girls, by as much as 9% at age 16, and the gap narrows to about 2.5% by 19 ([The Zebra](https://www.thezebra.com/resources/car-insurance/teen-driving-report/)).

## Why young drivers pay more

Insurers price by risk, and young drivers have little history and more crashes. Motor vehicle crashes are the second leading cause of death among teens, and a 16- or 17-year-old driver's risk of being killed rises with each teen passenger: 44% higher with one, double with two, and four times higher with three or more ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/background-on-teen-drivers)). Prices usually drop each year a young driver keeps a clean record.

## Add your teen to your policy, or get them their own?

For most families, **adding a teen to a parent's policy costs less** than buying them a separate one ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/auto-insurance-for-teen-drivers)). Even so, expect a big jump: adding a teenager can raise a parent's premium by **50% to 100%** ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/background-on-teen-drivers)). One analysis found parents save about $1,900 a year by adding a teen instead of buying a separate policy ([Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/do-permit-drivers-need-insurance/)).

If a new teen driver lives with you, they need to be insured, and insurers expect every household driver to be listed ([Texas Department of Insurance](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/adding-teen-driver-to-insurance-policy.html)). Leaving one out can lead an insurer to cancel or not renew your policy ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/article/consumer-insight-comparing-online-auto-insurance-quotes)).

## 8 ways to lower car insurance for a young driver

1. **Share a policy.** Add the teen to a parent's policy, and ask about a multi-car discount ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/auto-insurance-for-teen-drivers)).
2. **Good-student discount.** Many insurers reward a "B" average or better.
3. **Driver's education.** Finishing a recognized course often earns a discount.
4. **Away-at-school discount.** A student at school 100+ miles away without a car may pay less.
5. **Pick the right car.** An inexpensive car with a good safety rating usually costs less to insure ([Texas Department of Insurance](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/adding-teen-driver-to-insurance-policy.html)).
6. **Assign the teen to the cheapest car.** Some insurers price by car and driver, but the teen must then drive only that car.
7. **Try a driving app or device.** Safe-driving programs usually earn a discount if the teen drives carefully.
8. **Raise the deductible and compare insurers.** A higher deductible lowers the premium, and prices for young drivers vary widely between companies.

## Learner's permits

Most families don't pay more while a teen only has a permit, but you should **call your insurer before the permit**, because rules differ by company. Our [learner's permit guide](/car-insurance-learners-permit/) covers when to add a permit driver and what happens at the driving test.

## New driver, not a teen?

If you're an adult buying your first policy, little history usually means a higher price at first. Compare several insurers, ask about every discount, and keep a clean record. Each year without claims or tickets helps.

## Common questions

### How much is car insurance for an 18-year-old?
About $7,498 a year for full coverage or $2,300 for state minimum coverage on average, though prices vary a lot by state and insurer ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/average-rates-by-age.aspx)).

### Is it cheaper to add my teen to my policy or get them their own?
Usually cheaper to add them to yours ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/auto-insurance-for-teen-drivers)). Ask for quotes both ways, since some families with expensive policies find a separate policy competitive.

### How much does adding a teen raise my insurance?
Often 50% to 100% ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/background-on-teen-drivers)). Discounts and the car the teen drives can bring that down.

### At what age does car insurance go down?
Prices fall steadily through the teens and early 20s. On average, full coverage drops from about $10,387 at 16 to about $3,044 at 25 ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/average-rates-by-age.aspx)).

### When should I tell my insurer about my teen?
Before they start driving your car, even with a permit ([Texas Department of Insurance](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/adding-teen-driver-to-insurance-policy.html)).

### Which discount helps most?
It depends on the insurer. Good-student and driver's-ed discounts are common and can last for years.
