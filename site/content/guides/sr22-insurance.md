If a court or your state's DMV told you to get an SR-22, you are not out of options. An SR-22 is a certificate your insurer files with the state to prove you carry the required liability coverage ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22)). It is common after a DUI, driving without insurance, or a license suspension, and many insurers will file one for you.

[[genie]]

## What an SR-22 actually is

An SR-22 is not a type of insurance. It is a "certificate of financial responsibility" that your insurance company sends to the state for you, showing you carry at least your state's minimum coverage ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22)).

- **The insurer files it, not you.** You ask for an SR-22 when you buy or update a policy.
- **An insurance card is not enough.** Texas, for example, says an insurance card or policy "will not be accepted in place of" an SR-22 ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).
- **It doesn't restore your license by itself.** You still need to finish the other steps on your notice, such as paying a reinstatement fee ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).

## Who usually needs one

Common reasons include ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22); [Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)):

- A DUI or DWI, or another serious moving violation
- Several traffic violations in a short time
- A conviction for driving without insurance, or a crash without coverage
- A license suspension after a crash, or an unpaid court judgment from a crash

Your court or DMV paperwork will say whether you need an SR-22, and for how long. Florida uses a stricter form called an **FR-44** after a DUI, with much higher liability limits ([FLHSMV](https://www.flhsmv.gov/driver-licenses-id-cards/education-courses/dui-and-iid/dui-faqs)), and Virginia also uses FR-44s ([CarInsurance.com](https://carinsurance.com/fr-44-insurance-virginia.aspx)). See our [Florida FR-44 guide](/fr44-insurance/florida/).

## How long you need an SR-22

In most states it's required for **three years**, but the start date and rules vary, so check with your state ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22)). Texas, for example, requires two years ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676); see our [Texas SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/texas/)).

## What happens if your coverage lapses

If your policy is cancelled before the SR-22 period ends, your insurer must notify the state. That can lead to your license being suspended again, and you may have to start the SR-22 process over ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22)). The form insurers use to end a filing is often called an **SR-26** ([Agile Rates](https://agilerates.com/advice/auto/difference-between-sr-22-vs-sr-26)).

If money is tight, call your insurer before a payment is missed. A cheaper policy is better than a lapse.

## What an SR-22 costs

1. **The filing fee:** typically about $15 to $50, paid through your insurer ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)). Some insurers may also ask for a fee or part of the premium up front ([Nationwide](https://www.nationwide.com/lc/resources/auto-insurance/articles/what-is-an-sr-22)).
2. **The higher premium:** the bigger cost. One analysis found an average increase of about $322 a year (12%) on a full-coverage policy, ranging from $32 in California to $2,122 in Michigan ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)).

Prices vary a lot between insurers for SR-22 drivers, so comparing quotes matters.

## Non-owner SR-22 insurance

If you don't own a car, you may still need an SR-22 to get your license back. A **non-owner policy** covers you when you drive cars you don't own, and the insurer can file the SR-22 with it. Most national and regional insurers offer it ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)). More in our [non-owner SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/non-owner/).

## How to get an SR-22, step by step

1. **Read your notice.** Note your state, the reason, and how long the SR-22 is required.
2. **Find an insurer that files SR-22s.** Not all do. Genie can point you to quoting partners that work with drivers in your situation.
3. **Buy the policy and ask for the filing.** Tell the insurer you need an SR-22 (or FR-44) and in which state.
4. **Confirm the state received it,** then finish any reinstatement steps.
5. **Keep the policy active for the whole period.** Automatic payments help.

## Common questions

### Can I get an SR-22 if my license is suspended?
Often, yes. Many insurers will issue a policy and file the SR-22 while your license is suspended, since it's commonly required before reinstatement, though not every insurer does ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)).

### Do I need an SR-22 if I move to another state?
Usually yes, until the original state's requirement ends. Tell your insurer before you move so it can keep the filing in place with the state that ordered it.

### Will an SR-22 show up forever?
No. Once the required period ends, the filing can be removed. The violation behind it may still affect your rate for a few years.

### Is SR-22 insurance more expensive?
The filing is cheap. The violation behind it is what raises your price, and how much depends heavily on the insurer.
