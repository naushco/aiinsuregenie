If a court or your state's DMV told you to get an SR-22, you are not out of options. An SR-22 is a form your insurer files with the state to prove you carry the required liability coverage. It is common after a DUI, driving without insurance, or a license suspension, and many insurers will file one for you.

[[genie]]

## What an SR-22 actually is

An SR-22 is not a type of insurance. It is a certificate, sometimes called a "certificate of financial responsibility," that your insurance company sends to the state on your behalf. It tells the state you have at least the minimum liability coverage required where you live.

A few things to know:

- **The insurer files it, not you.** You ask for an SR-22 when you buy or update a policy, and the insurer sends it to the state.
- **An insurance card is not enough.** States want the SR-22 filing itself, not a copy of your policy.
- **It doesn't restore your license by itself.** You still need to complete any other steps the state lists, such as paying a reinstatement fee.

## Who usually needs one

The exact rules depend on your state, but these are the most common reasons:

- A DUI or DWI conviction
- Driving without insurance, especially more than once
- A license suspension after a serious violation or an at-fault crash without insurance
- An unpaid court judgment from a crash
- Too many points or serious tickets in a short time

Your court or DMV paperwork will say whether you need an SR-22, and for how long. Florida and Virginia also use a stricter form called an **FR-44** for some offenses, with higher liability limits (see our [Florida FR-44 guide](/fr44-insurance/florida/)).

## How long you need an SR-22

Most states require an SR-22 for about **three years**, but it can be shorter or longer. Texas, for example, usually requires two years (see our [Texas SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/texas/)). The clock often starts from the conviction date or the date your license is reinstated, so check your notice.

The filing usually renews on its own while your policy stays active. You don't need to refile it each year.

## What happens if your coverage lapses

This is the part to be careful about. If your policy is cancelled or you miss a payment, your insurer is required to tell the state, often with a form called an **SR-26**. The state can then suspend your license again, and in some states the SR-22 period starts over.

If money is tight, call your insurer before a payment is missed. Switching to a cheaper policy is better than letting coverage lapse.

## What an SR-22 costs

There are two costs:

1. **The filing fee:** usually about $15 to $50, paid once through your insurer.
2. **The higher premium:** the bigger cost. The violation behind the SR-22 is what raises your rate, not the form itself. One national estimate puts the increase at about 12% on a full-coverage policy on average, with large differences between states.

Prices vary a lot between insurers for SR-22 drivers. That is why comparing quotes matters more for you than for almost anyone else.

## Non-owner SR-22 insurance

If you don't own a car, you may still need an SR-22 to get your license back. A **non-owner policy** covers you when you drive a car you don't own, such as a borrowed or rented car, and the insurer can file the SR-22 with it. It is usually cheaper than insuring a car. Most national and regional insurers offer it, but not all of them, so ask directly. More in our [non-owner SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/non-owner/).

## How to get an SR-22, step by step

1. **Read your notice.** Note your state, the reason, and how long the SR-22 is required.
2. **Find an insurer that files SR-22s.** Some insurers don't, or only for existing customers. Genie can point you to quoting partners that work with drivers in your situation.
3. **Buy the policy and ask for the filing.** Tell the insurer you need an SR-22 (or FR-44) and in which state.
4. **Confirm the state received it.** Ask the insurer for confirmation, then finish any reinstatement steps your state lists.
5. **Keep the policy active for the whole period.** Set up automatic payments if you can.

## Common questions

### Can I get an SR-22 if my license is suspended?
Often, yes. Many insurers will sell you a policy and file the SR-22 while your license is suspended, because the filing is usually required before reinstatement. Not every insurer does this, so ask before you buy.

### Do I need an SR-22 if I move to another state?
Usually yes, until the original state's requirement ends. Your new insurer must be able to file in the state that ordered it. Tell the insurer about the move before you switch.

### Will an SR-22 show up forever?
No. Once the required period ends and your insurer files to end it, the SR-22 drops off. The violation itself may still affect your rate for a few years.

### Is SR-22 insurance more expensive?
The filing is cheap. The violation behind it is what raises your price, and how much depends heavily on the insurer, so compare several quotes.
