**Quick answer:** every state except New Hampshire requires car insurance, and most set liability minimums around **$25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident** for injuries plus **$10,000 to $25,000** for property damage. Some states also require personal injury protection (PIP) or uninsured motorist (UM) coverage. Florida doesn't require bodily injury liability at all, while Alaska, Maine, Michigan, North Carolina and Virginia require $50,000 / $100,000 ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/auto/state-minimum-car-insurance-requirements/), verified May 2026).

## How to read the table

Limits are written as **bodily injury per person / per accident**, then **property damage**. Texas's 30/60/25, for example, means your insurer pays up to $30,000 for one person's injuries, $60,000 for everyone hurt in one crash, and $25,000 for property you damage.

- **PIP** (personal injury protection) pays your own medical bills no matter who caused the crash. States that require it are often called no-fault states.
- **UM/UIM** (uninsured / underinsured motorist) pays you when the other driver has no insurance or too little.
- **Proof filing** is the form your insurer files with the state after serious violations. Most states use an [SR-22](/sr22-insurance/). Florida and Virginia also use the stricter FR-44 for DUI-related convictions ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/insurance/car-insurance-basics/sr22-insurance/)).

## Is the state minimum enough?

The minimum keeps you legal, but it often won't cover a serious crash. Medical bills and newer cars can easily cost more than $25,000, and you're personally responsible for the rest. Many drivers choose higher limits, such as 100/300/100. Minimums also don't cover damage to your own car. That needs collision and comprehensive coverage, which lenders require on financed cars.

## What happens if you drive without the minimum?

Penalties depend on the state but can include fines, a suspended license or registration, and an SR-22 requirement. A gap in coverage also usually raises your next price. If your coverage has lapsed, see our guide to [car insurance after a lapse](/car-insurance-after-lapse/).

## Common questions

### What is the minimum car insurance in most states?
Most states require about $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for injuries, plus $10,000 to $25,000 for property damage ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/auto/state-minimum-car-insurance-requirements/)).

### Which state doesn't require car insurance?
New Hampshire doesn't require you to buy car insurance, but you must still be able to pay for damage you cause, and the listed amounts apply if you do buy a policy ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/auto/state-minimum-car-insurance-requirements/)).

### Which states have the highest minimums?
Alaska, Maine, Michigan, North Carolina and Virginia require $50,000 / $100,000 for bodily injury, and North Carolina also requires $50,000 for property damage ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/auto/state-minimum-car-insurance-requirements/)).

### Which states use FR-44 instead of SR-22?
Only Florida and Virginia, for DUI-related convictions. See our [Florida](/fr44-insurance/florida/) and [Virginia](/fr44-insurance/virginia/) FR-44 guides.

### Does minimum coverage pay for my own car?
No. Liability pays for damage you cause to others. Collision and comprehensive cover your own car.
