A lapse in car insurance happens to a lot of drivers, and you can fix it. The most important thing is to **get covered again quickly**. One recent analysis found rates rose about **8%** after a lapse of 30 days or less, but about **35%** after a lapse longer than 30 days.

[[genie]]

## What counts as a lapse

A lapse is any time your car is registered but not insured, even for one day. Common causes:

- A missed payment that led to cancellation
- A renewal you forgot about
- Selling a car and not buying a new policy right away
- Stopping driving for a while without pausing coverage properly

## What can happen after a lapse

- **Higher rates.** Insurers see a gap as higher risk. Longer gaps cost more.
- **State penalties.** Many insurers report cancellations to the state. Depending on where you live, that can mean fines, a suspended registration or license, or an SR-22 requirement. In Florida, for example, reinstatement fees run $150 for a first lapse up to $500 for a third.
- **Fewer choices.** Some insurers won't quote drivers with a recent gap. Others specialize in it.

## How to get covered again

1. **Call your old insurer first.** For short lapses, some will reinstate your policy with little or no penalty.
2. **Don't wait.** The penalty grows the longer you're uninsured, so get any policy in place now and shop for a better one after.
3. **Be honest about the gap.** Insurers check records, and hiding a lapse can get a policy cancelled.
4. **Compare several insurers.** Some insurers barely penalize short lapses, while others raise rates sharply.
5. **Check your state's rules.** If you got a notice, you may need to show proof of insurance or file an [SR-22](/sr22-insurance/).

## How to keep it from happening again

- Turn on automatic payments
- Ask about a grace period for late payments
- If you're not driving for a while, ask about pausing coverage rather than cancelling
- Buy your new policy before cancelling the old one

## Common questions

### How long can I go without car insurance before rates go up?
Some insurers don't penalize gaps under about two weeks. After 30 days, most do, and the increase is usually bigger.

### Will a lapse stay on my record?
Insurers typically look at whether you were continuously insured over the last few years. Each month of continuous coverage after the gap helps.

### Can I get insurance the same day after a lapse?
Usually yes. Many insurers can start a policy the same day, even after a gap.
