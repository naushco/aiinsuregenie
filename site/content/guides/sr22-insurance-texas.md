In Texas, an SR-22 proves to the Department of Public Safety (DPS) that you carry the state's minimum liability insurance. DPS requires most drivers who need one to keep it for **two years**, and the insurer files it for you ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)). New to SR-22s? Start with our [complete SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/).

## Who needs an SR-22 in Texas

According to [Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676), you must file an SR-22 if:

- Your driving privilege was suspended because of a crash
- You have a second or later conviction for driving without liability insurance (it can be waived if you had insurance at the time and submit proof)
- A civil judgment from a crash was filed against you
- A security deposit is being placed with DPS for a crash-related suspension

Other suspensions can also come with an SR-22 requirement. Your DPS or court notice is the final word, so read it closely.

## Texas minimum coverage for an SR-22

Your policy must meet at least the Texas minimums, often written as **30/60/25** ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676); [Texas Department of Insurance](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/car-insurance.html)):

| Coverage | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Bodily injury, one person | $30,000 |
| Bodily injury, two or more people | $60,000 |
| Property damage | $25,000 |

Many drivers choose higher limits, because the minimums can run out quickly in a serious crash.

## How long you need it in Texas

DPS requires you to keep the SR-22 for **two years** from the conviction date, or from the date a crash-related judgment was made ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).

## If your SR-22 lapses in Texas

Insurers automatically tell DPS when a policy is cancelled or lapses. If the SR-22 isn't on file, or it lapses and a new one isn't filed in time, DPS can suspend your **license and your vehicle registration** ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)). If you switch insurers, make sure the new SR-22 is filed before the old policy ends.

## Costs in Texas

- **Reinstatement fee:** DPS lists a $100 reinstatement fee, plus any other fees you owe ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).
- **Filing fee:** insurers typically charge about $15 to $50 to file an SR-22 ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)).
- **Your premium:** the biggest cost, and it varies a lot by insurer. Compare several quotes.

## Non-owner SR-22 in Texas

If you don't own a car, DPS suggests asking an insurer about a **Texas non-owner SR-22 policy** ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)). See our [non-owner SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/non-owner/).

## Steps to get your SR-22 in Texas

1. Check your DPS notice for the reason and the dates.
2. Find an insurer that files SR-22s in Texas. Genie can point you to a quoting partner.
3. Buy at least 30/60/25 coverage and ask the insurer to file the SR-22 with DPS.
4. Pay the $100 reinstatement fee and any other fees DPS lists.
5. Keep the policy active for the full two years.

## Common questions

### Does an insurance card count instead of an SR-22 in Texas?
No. DPS says an insurance card or policy will not be accepted in place of an SR-22 ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).

### Can I get a Texas SR-22 without a car?
Yes, through a non-owner policy, which DPS mentions as an option ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).

### When does my Texas SR-22 requirement end?
Two years from the conviction date, or from the date of the crash-related judgment ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).
