In Texas, an SR-22 proves to the Department of Public Safety (DPS) that you carry the state's minimum liability insurance. Most Texas drivers who need one must keep it for **two years**, and the insurer files it for you. New to SR-22s? Start with our [complete SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/).

## Who needs an SR-22 in Texas

According to Texas DPS, you must file an SR-22 if:

- Your driving privilege was suspended because of a crash
- You have a second or later conviction for driving without liability insurance (the requirement can be waived if you had insurance at the time and submit proof)
- A civil judgment from a crash was filed against you
- A security deposit is being placed with DPS for a crash-related suspension

Other suspensions, such as some DWI cases, often come with an SR-22 requirement too. Your DPS or court notice is the final word, so read it closely.

## Texas minimum coverage for an SR-22

Your policy must meet at least the Texas minimums, often written as **30/60/25**:

| Coverage | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| Bodily injury, one person | $30,000 |
| Bodily injury, two or more people | $60,000 |
| Property damage | $25,000 |

Many drivers choose higher limits, because the minimums can run out quickly in a serious crash.

## How long you need it in Texas

DPS requires you to keep the SR-22 for **two years**. The two years count from the conviction date, or from the date a crash-related judgment was made. Keep your policy active the whole time.

## If your SR-22 lapses in Texas

Insurers automatically tell DPS when a policy is cancelled or lapses. If you're required to have an SR-22 and it isn't on file, or it lapses and a new one isn't filed in time, DPS can suspend your **license and your vehicle registration**. If you switch insurers, make sure the new SR-22 is filed before the old policy ends.

## Costs in Texas

- **Reinstatement fee:** DPS lists a $100 reinstatement fee, plus any other fees you owe.
- **Filing fee:** your insurer may charge a small fee to file the SR-22, often about $15 to $50.
- **Your premium:** the biggest cost, and it varies a lot by insurer. Compare several quotes.

## Non-owner SR-22 in Texas

If you don't own a car, Texas DPS suggests asking an insurer about a **Texas non-owner SR-22 policy**. It covers you when you drive cars you don't own, and lets you meet the SR-22 requirement without insuring a vehicle.

## Steps to get your SR-22 in Texas

1. Check your DPS notice for the reason and the dates.
2. Find an insurer that files SR-22s in Texas. Genie can point you to a quoting partner.
3. Buy at least 30/60/25 coverage and ask the insurer to file the SR-22 with DPS.
4. Pay the $100 reinstatement fee and any other fees DPS lists.
5. Keep the policy active for the full two years.

## Common questions

### Does an insurance card count instead of an SR-22 in Texas?
No. DPS says an insurance card or policy will not be accepted in place of an SR-22. The insurer must file the certificate.

### Can I get a Texas SR-22 without a car?
Yes, through a non-owner policy. Ask the insurer to file the SR-22 with it.

### When does my Texas SR-22 requirement end?
Two years from the conviction date, or from the date of the crash-related judgment. After that, ask your insurer whether the filing can be removed.
