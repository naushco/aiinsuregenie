If you need an SR-22 to get your license back but you don't own a car, a **non-owner SR-22 policy** is usually the answer. It gives you liability coverage when you drive cars you don't own, and the insurer files the SR-22 with your state. It typically costs less than insuring a car.

[[genie]]

## Who needs a non-owner SR-22

It fits drivers who:

- Were told to file an SR-22 after a DUI, driving uninsured, or too many violations
- Don't own a car, or sold their car after the suspension
- Borrow or rent cars now and then, or want their license back before buying a car

If you own a car, you need a regular policy on that car with the SR-22 attached instead. See our [complete SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/) for the basics.

## What it covers

- **Liability** for injuries and property damage you cause while driving a car you don't own, at least at your state's minimum limits
- **Personal injury protection or uninsured motorist coverage,** where your state requires them
- **The SR-22 filing** the state asked for

It is usually **secondary**: if you borrow a friend's car, their insurance pays first, and yours helps after their limits run out.

## What it doesn't cover

- **Damage to the car you're driving.** There is no collision or comprehensive coverage for the borrowed car.
- **Your own injuries,** unless your policy includes personal injury protection.
- **A car you own or drive regularly.** Insurers generally won't sell a non-owner policy for a car that lives at your home or that you use every day. Ask before you buy.

## What it costs

One national estimate puts a non-owner SR-22 at about **$380 a year** on average for a clean record, ranging from roughly $179 a year in Iowa to $668 in Florida. A DUI pushes it much higher, so comparing insurers matters. The SR-22 filing fee itself is usually about $15 to $50.

## How to get one

1. **Check your notice** for how long you need the SR-22. It's often about three years.
2. **Find an insurer that sells non-owner policies and files SR-22s.** Many don't sell them online, so it often takes a call. Genie can point you to a quoting partner.
3. **Ask for the SR-22 filing** when you buy, and confirm the state received it.
4. **Keep it active the whole time.** If it lapses, your insurer must tell the state.
5. **Switch to a regular policy** with the SR-22 attached if you buy a car.

## Common questions

### Can I get a non-owner SR-22 with a suspended license?
Often, yes. Many insurers will sell the policy and file the SR-22 while your license is suspended, because the filing is usually needed for reinstatement.

### Does a non-owner policy cover rental cars?
Usually it gives you liability coverage in a rental, but not damage to the rental car itself. Check the policy, or buy the rental company's damage waiver.

### What if I buy a car later?
Tell your insurer right away. You'll need a regular policy on that car, with the SR-22 moved to it, and there should be no gap in coverage.
