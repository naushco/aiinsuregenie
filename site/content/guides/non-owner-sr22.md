If you need an SR-22 to get your license back but you don't own a car, a **non-owner SR-22 policy** is usually the answer. It gives you liability coverage when you drive cars you don't own, and the insurer files the SR-22 with your state ([Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance)). Texas DPS, for example, points drivers without a car to this option ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/node/676)).

[[genie]]

## Who needs a non-owner SR-22

It fits drivers who:

- Were told to file an SR-22 after a DUI, driving uninsured, or too many violations
- Don't own a car, or sold their car after the suspension
- Borrow or rent cars now and then, or want their license back before buying a car

If you own or lease a car, you'll usually need a standard policy on that car instead ([InsuranceQuotes](https://www.insurancequotes.com/auto/non-owner-car-insurance-guide)). See our [complete SR-22 guide](/sr22-insurance/) for the basics.

## What it covers

Per [Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance):

- **Liability** for injuries and property damage you cause while driving a car you don't own, at least at your state's minimum limits
- **Personal injury protection or uninsured motorist coverage,** where your state requires them
- It's **secondary**: the car owner's insurance pays first, and yours helps after their limits run out

## What it doesn't cover

- **The car you're driving.** It doesn't pay for damage to the borrowed car ([Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance)), including a rental car ([InsuranceQuotes](https://www.insurancequotes.com/auto/non-owner-car-insurance-guide)).
- **Your own injuries,** unless personal injury protection is included ([Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance)).
- **A household car you use regularly.** Insurers typically want you not to own a car and not to have regular access to a household vehicle ([InsuranceQuotes](https://www.insurancequotes.com/auto/non-owner-car-insurance-guide)).

## What it costs

One analysis puts a non-owner SR-22 at about **$380 a year** on average for a clean record, ranging from about $179 in Iowa to $668 in Florida, and much higher after a DUI ([Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance)). The filing fee itself is typically about $15 to $50 ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)).

## How to get one

1. **Check your notice** for how long you need the SR-22.
2. **Find an insurer that sells non-owner policies and files SR-22s.** These are usually not sold online, so it often takes a call ([Insurance.com](https://insurance.com/auto-insurance/non-owner-car-insurance/non-owner-sr22-insurance)). Genie can point you to a quoting partner.
3. **Ask for the SR-22 filing** when you buy, and confirm the state received it.
4. **Keep it active the whole time.** If it lapses, your insurer must tell the state.
5. **Switch to a regular policy** with the SR-22 attached if you buy a car.

## Common questions

### Can I get a non-owner SR-22 with a suspended license?
Often, yes. Many insurers will sell the policy and file the SR-22 while your license is suspended ([CarInsurance.com](https://www.carinsurance.com/kb/how-to-get-sr22-without-valid-license)).

### Does a non-owner policy cover rental cars?
It can give you liability coverage, but typically not damage to the rental car itself. For that, consider the rental company's damage waiver ([InsuranceQuotes](https://www.insurancequotes.com/auto/non-owner-car-insurance-guide)).

### What if I buy a car later?
Tell your insurer right away. You'll need a standard policy on that car with the SR-22 on it, and no gap in coverage.
