**Quick answer:** adding a 16-year-old to a parent's policy raised the average full-coverage premium from **$2,060 to $5,312 a year**, a **158% increase**, in one national analysis ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)). More recent data puts the average full-coverage rate for a 16-year-old on a parent's policy at about **$5,740 a year**, or roughly $270 more a month ([Bankrate via KTTC, April 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/04/02/adding-teen-driver-auto-insurance-comes-with-major-price-jump/)). It's still usually much cheaper than a separate policy.

## Cost of adding a young driver, by age

Average yearly full-coverage premium for a married couple, before and after adding a child ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)):

| Child's age | Before | After | Increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | $2,060 | $5,312 | +$3,252 (158%) |
| 21 | $2,060 | $4,212 | +$2,152 (105%) |
| 25 | $2,060 | $2,581 | +$521 (25%) |

The jump shrinks quickly as your child gains years of clean driving. The Insurance Information Institute puts the typical increase at **50% to 100%** ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/background-on-teen-drivers)), depending on the insurer, state and car.

## Adding a teen vs. a separate policy

Adding a teen to a parent's policy instead of buying a separate teen policy saves about **62%** on average ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)). The parent's longer history, multi-car discounts and bundling usually outweigh the teen's risk.

## Where it costs most and least

For a 16-year-old, the biggest increase in that study was **Rhode Island** (+225%, about $5,828 a year). The smallest was **Hawaii** (+4.7%, about $74), because Hawaii doesn't let insurers price by age or credit score ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)).

## How to lower the cost

1. **Good-student discount:** up to 25% at some insurers for a B average or better. See our [good student discount guide](/good-student-discount-car-insurance/).
2. **Choose the car carefully:** a modest car with good safety ratings is cheaper to insure than a new or sporty one.
3. **Driver's education** and **safe-driving apps** often earn discounts.
4. **Raise deductibles** on the teen's car, or drop collision on an older car.
5. **Compare insurers** before adding your teen. Increases vary a lot by company.

## Common questions

### How much does it cost to add a 16-year-old to car insurance?
About $3,250 more a year on average for full coverage (from $2,060 to $5,312) in one national analysis ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)), though it varies widely by state and insurer.

### Is it cheaper to add a teen to my policy or buy their own?
Usually cheaper to add them, by about 62% on average (same source).

### When should I add my teen to my car insurance?
Call your insurer before they get a permit. Many insurers don't raise the price until the teen is licensed. See our [learner's permit guide](/car-insurance-learners-permit/).

### Which state is cheapest to add a teen driver?
Hawaii, where adding a 16-year-old raised premiums by under 5% because age can't be used in pricing ([ValuePenguin](https://www.valuepenguin.com/cost-of-adding-young-driver-to-family-car-insurance)).
